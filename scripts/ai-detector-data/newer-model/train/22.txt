Once upon a time, in a forest where the trees grew so tall they seemed to touch the clouds, there lived a small fox named Pip.

Pip was smaller than all the other foxes, and his tail was a little too short. The other animals sometimes laughed at him, and Pip often wished he could be big and strong like the bears or fast and graceful like the deer.

One winter, a terrible storm swept through the forest. Snow piled high against the trees, and the wind howled through the branches. When the storm finally passed, the animals discovered that a baby rabbit named Clover had gone missing.

The bears searched, but they were too big to fit between the fallen logs. The deer searched, but their long legs sank deep into the snow. Everyone began to lose hope.

Then Pip had an idea. Because he was so small, he could squeeze into tiny spaces that no one else could reach. He wriggled under branches and crawled through narrow tunnels in the snow, calling Clover's name.

At last, he heard a tiny voice. There, curled up beneath a hollow log, was Clover—cold and frightened, but safe.

When Pip brought her home, all the animals cheered. From that day on, no one laughed at Pip again. And Pip learned that being different wasn't a weakness at all. Sometimes, it was exactly what the world needed.
