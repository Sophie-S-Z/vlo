The first thing Mira noticed about the new girl was her shoes: bright yellow rain boots, even though it hadn't rained in weeks.

The second thing she noticed was that the new girl sat alone at lunch, her tray untouched, staring out the cafeteria window like she was waiting for something.

Mira told herself it was none of her business. She had her own problems—a math test on Friday, a mother who worked double shifts, a best friend who had stopped answering her texts. She didn't need to adopt anyone.

But on the third day, when a group of eighth graders started laughing at the boots, Mira found herself standing up before she could think it through.

"They're cool," she said loudly, sliding into the seat across from the new girl. "Where'd you get them?"

The girl looked up, startled. Up close, her eyes were red, like she'd been crying. "My dad," she said quietly. "Before he left."

Mira didn't know what to say to that, so she said nothing. She just opened her bag of chips and set it in the middle of the table, an offering.

After a moment, the girl took one. Then another.

"I'm Juniper," she said.

"Mira."

It wasn't much. But sometimes, Mira thought later, friendship starts with something small—a bag of chips, a pair of yellow boots, and the courage to sit down.
