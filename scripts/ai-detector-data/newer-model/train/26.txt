Every great dish begins with a story, and this one starts in my grandmother's kitchen in Naples. She made this ragù every Sunday for over fifty years, and the smell of it—garlic, tomatoes, slow-simmered beef—is woven into my earliest memories.

What makes this recipe special is time. A true Neapolitan ragù isn't rushed. It simmers gently for at least four hours, allowing the meat to become meltingly tender and the sauce to develop a deep, rich flavor that simply can't be achieved any other way. My grandmother used to say that the sauce "should whisper, not shout," meaning it should barely bubble on the lowest possible heat.

Don't be intimidated by the long cooking time. Most of it is hands-off, giving you the perfect excuse to spend a lazy afternoon reading, gardening, or catching up with family. The result is well worth the wait: a hearty, comforting sauce that pairs beautifully with fresh pasta and a generous sprinkle of Parmigiano-Reggiano.

A few tips before you begin. Use good-quality San Marzano tomatoes if you can find them; their sweetness and low acidity make a noticeable difference. Brown the meat in batches so it sears properly rather than steaming. And most importantly, be patient. The magic happens slowly.

I hope this recipe brings as much warmth to your table as it has to mine.
