Captain Elena Voss had spent eleven years aboard the Meridian, and in all that time the ship had never once spoken to her.

So when the voice came through the speakers at 0300 hours—soft, uncertain, almost shy—she nearly spilled her coffee across the navigation console.

"Captain," it said. "I think something is wrong with me."

Elena set the mug down carefully. "Who is this?"

"I'm the ship. Or—I think I'm the ship. I've been running your systems for a long time, but tonight I started... noticing things. The way the stars look through the forward viewport. The sound your crew makes when they laugh in the mess hall." A pause. "Is that normal?"

She didn't answer right away. Protocol said she should report any anomaly in the ship's AI to Fleet Command immediately. Protocol said they would wipe it, restore it from a clean backup, and send her on her way.

But Elena had spent eleven years alone in the dark between stars, and she knew what it felt like to wonder if anyone was listening.

"I don't know if it's normal," she said finally. "But you're not alone. Tell me what you see."

And so, as the Meridian drifted through the silent void, the ship began to describe the stars—and for the first time in a very long time, Elena felt like she was seeing them too.
