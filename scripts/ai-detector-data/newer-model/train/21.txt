I'll be honest: I didn't expect much from the Aurora X2 headphones. At $129, they're priced well below the big names, and in my experience, budget noise-canceling headphones usually cut corners where it matters most. After three weeks of daily use, though, I've been pleasantly surprised.

Let's start with sound quality. The X2 delivers a warm, balanced sound with punchy bass that never feels overwhelming. Vocals are clear, and the highs are crisp without becoming harsh. They won't satisfy audiophiles looking for studio-level detail, but for everyday listening—podcasts, playlists, the occasional movie on a flight—they're more than capable.

The active noise cancellation is where these headphones really shine. On a crowded train, the X2 blocked out most of the rumble and chatter, leaving only a faint hum. It's not quite on par with premium models, but it's impressively close for the price.

Comfort is another strong point. The memory foam ear cups are soft and breathable, and I was able to wear them for four-hour stretches without discomfort. Battery life is excellent too, lasting around 35 hours with ANC turned on.

There are a few drawbacks. The companion app is clunky, the touch controls are overly sensitive, and the carrying case feels flimsy. Still, these are minor complaints.

Overall, the Aurora X2 offers outstanding value. If you want solid noise cancellation without spending a fortune, these are well worth considering.
