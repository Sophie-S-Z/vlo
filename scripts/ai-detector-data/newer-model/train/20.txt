Lily had always believed that love was something that happened to other people.

She was thirty-four, worked as a librarian in a small town in Vermont, and spent most of her evenings curled up with a novel and a cup of chamomile tea. She liked her life. It was quiet, predictable, and safe.

Then, one rainy Tuesday in October, a stranger walked into the library looking for a book about lighthouses.

He was tall, with rain-soaked hair and kind gray eyes, and he apologized three times for dripping on the carpet. His name was Daniel. He was restoring an old lighthouse on the coast, he explained, and he wanted to understand its history before he changed anything.

Lily found him the book. Then she found him three more. By the time the library closed, they had been talking for two hours, and she realized with a start that she hadn't looked at the clock once.

He came back the next day, and the day after that. They talked about books, about the sea, about the strange comfort of old buildings. Slowly, almost without noticing, Lily began to look forward to the sound of the door opening each afternoon.

She had always thought love would arrive like a storm. Instead, it came quietly—like a light on the horizon, steady and patient, guiding her home.
