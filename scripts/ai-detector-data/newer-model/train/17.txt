The house at the end of Willow Lane had been empty for as long as anyone could remember. The windows were dark, the garden overgrown, and the front door hung slightly ajar, as if someone had left in a hurry and never come back.

Of course, that was exactly why Sam and her friends decided to go inside.

It was Halloween night, and the air was crisp and cold. Their flashlights cut thin beams through the darkness as they crept up the creaking porch steps. Somewhere in the distance, a dog barked, then fell silent.

"This is a bad idea," whispered Theo, clutching the strap of his backpack.

"You say that about everything," Sam replied, pushing the door open.

The hallway smelled of dust and something else—something sweet and rotten, like flowers left too long in a vase. Faded wallpaper peeled from the walls. A grandfather clock stood at the far end, its pendulum perfectly still.

And then, as they stood frozen in the doorway, the clock began to tick.

Sam's heart pounded in her chest. Theo grabbed her arm. Behind them, slowly and deliberately, the front door swung shut with a soft, final click.

For a moment, no one moved. Then, from somewhere upstairs, they heard footsteps—slow, heavy, and coming closer.
