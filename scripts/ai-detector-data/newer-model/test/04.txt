The dragon had been asleep beneath the mountain for three hundred years when Wren came looking for it.

She was not a knight. She did not have a sword, or armor, or a horse. She had a satchel full of bread and cheese, a pair of boots that pinched at the heel, and a question she needed answered. The village elders had told her the dragon was dangerous. They had also told her that it was the only creature alive old enough to remember the name of the river before it was poisoned.

The tunnel narrowed as she went deeper. The air grew warm and smelled of smoke and old copper. Somewhere ahead, she could hear breathing, slow and enormous, like the tide coming in.

When she finally stepped into the cavern, the dragon opened one eye. It was the color of molten gold, and it regarded her with an expression she could only describe as tired.

"Another hero," it said. Its voice rumbled through the stone beneath her feet. "Let me guess. You want my treasure."

"No," Wren said. Her voice shook, but she kept going. "I want to know what the river was called. Before."

The dragon was silent for a long moment. Then, slowly, it lifted its great head, and for the first time in centuries, it smiled.

"No one has asked me that," it said, "in a very long time."

It told her the name. It told her much more besides. And when Wren finally climbed back into the daylight, two days later, she carried something far more valuable than gold.
