Detective Ana Ruiz had seen a lot of crime scenes, but she had never seen one this clean.

The apartment on Delancey Street looked like a showroom. The kitchen counters gleamed. The books on the shelves were arranged by color. Even the body of Martin Hale, sixty-two, retired accountant, lay perfectly straight on the living room rug, his hands folded on his chest as if someone had arranged him for a viewing.

"No forced entry," said Officer Kim, flipping through his notebook. "Neighbors didn't hear anything. Cleaner comes on Thursdays, but today's Tuesday."

Ana crouched beside the body. There were no visible wounds, no signs of struggle. But something about the scene bothered her, a detail just out of reach, like a word on the tip of her tongue.

She stood and walked slowly around the room. The window was locked. The radiator ticked. On the coffee table, a single glass sat on a coaster, half full of water.

That was it. She leaned closer. Everything in the apartment had been cleaned, polished, and put away, except this glass. Whoever had done this had been careful. Meticulous. And yet they had left one thing behind.

"Bag the glass," she said. "And find out who his cleaner is."

Kim raised an eyebrow. "You think the cleaner did it?"

"I think," Ana said, "that someone wanted this place to look like nothing happened. And people who want that usually have a very good reason."
