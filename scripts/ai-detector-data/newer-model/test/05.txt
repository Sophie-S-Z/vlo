Remote work offers employees flexibility, but it also blurs the line between professional and personal life. Without a commute or a physical office, many workers find it difficult to know when the workday ends. Emails arrive late at night, meetings stretch into dinner, and the laptop on the kitchen table becomes a constant reminder of unfinished tasks.

There are several practical strategies that can help. First, set clear working hours and communicate them to your team. When colleagues know you log off at six, they are less likely to expect an immediate reply in the evening. Second, create a dedicated workspace, even if it is just a corner of a room. Physically leaving that space at the end of the day signals to your brain that work is over. Third, build small rituals that mark the transition, such as a short walk, a change of clothes, or a few minutes of stretching.

Managers also play an important role. They can model healthy boundaries by avoiding after-hours messages and by respecting employees' time off. Some companies have adopted policies that delay the delivery of emails sent outside working hours, which reduces pressure without limiting flexibility.

Finally, it is worth remembering that boundaries are not a sign of low commitment. Rested employees tend to be more creative, more focused, and less likely to burn out. Protecting your personal time is not only good for you; it is good for the work itself.
