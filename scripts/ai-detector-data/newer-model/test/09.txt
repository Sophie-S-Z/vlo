My sister texts me photos of her garden every Sunday. Tomatoes mostly, some squash, a sunflower she's very proud of that leans like it's had a few drinks. I live in a fourth-floor walkup with one window that faces a brick wall, so I think she does it partly to rub it in.

I tried to grow basil last spring. Bought the pot, the soil, a little bag of seeds from the hardware store. It came up fast, which surprised me, and for about three weeks I was insufferable about it. Then I went to Ohio for my cousin's wedding and forgot to ask anyone to water it. When I got back it was brown and crisp and I threw the whole thing out, pot and all, because I didn't want to look at it.

She laughed when I told her. Then she mailed me a jar of pesto.

I've been thinking about why I quit so fast. I don't think it was the basil. I think I'd decided before I even started that I was the kind of person who kills plants, and the first dead one just proved me right. That's a dumb way to live, and I do it about a lot of things.

So this year I bought two pots. If one dies, fine. I'll still have the other one, and I'll have learned something about how much water is too much. My sister says that's the whole secret anyway. Most people don't kill plants by forgetting them. They drown them.
