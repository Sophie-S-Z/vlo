When I was nine, my father taught me to fish on a lake so still that it held the whole sky. We would leave the house before dawn, the truck heater rattling, a thermos of cocoa wedged between the seats. He never talked much on those mornings. I think he liked the quiet more than the fishing.

I was a terrible fisherman. I tangled my line, lost lures in the reeds, and once dropped an entire rod over the side of the boat. My father never got angry. He would just untangle the mess with his big, patient hands and pass it back to me without a word.

It took me years to understand what those mornings were really about. My father worked long shifts at the mill, and during the week I barely saw him. The lake was the one place where he had nothing to do but be with me. The fish were almost beside the point.

He has been gone for eleven years now. Sometimes, when I cannot sleep, I drive out to that same lake and sit on the dock until the sun comes up. I still do not catch much. But I have come to love the quiet the way he did, and in the stillness, I can almost hear the rattle of that old truck heater, and feel him sitting beside me, saying nothing at all.
