The city of Veyra floated three miles above the clouds, held aloft by a lattice of ancient engines that no one living knew how to repair.

Tomas had been an engine-tender for six years, long enough to know that the hum beneath the streets was getting quieter. Each month, another district went dark. Each month, the Council issued another statement assuring citizens that everything was under control.

He did not believe them. Neither did his mentor, old Sefa, who had spent forty years crawling through the maintenance shafts and who now spent most of her days staring at a map she had drawn herself, covered in notes and arrows and question marks.

"There's a room," she told him one night, tapping the center of the map with a crooked finger. "At the heart of the lattice. The original builders sealed it. If there's a way to fix this, it's there."

"And if there isn't?"

Sefa looked at him for a long moment. In the lamplight, her eyes were sharp and tired. "Then at least we'll know," she said. "That's more than the Council can say."

They set out three days later, carrying rope, lamps, and a set of tools that had not been used since before either of them was born. The shafts grew narrower and hotter as they descended, and the hum grew louder, until it felt less like a sound and more like a heartbeat.

At the bottom, they found the door. It was not locked. It was simply waiting, as if it had always known someone would come.
