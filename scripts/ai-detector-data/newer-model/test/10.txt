The bus was late again, so Dev walked. It was eleven blocks to the hospital and his knee hurt by the sixth, but he didn't want to stand at the shelter with the man who kept asking everyone for a lighter.

His mother was asleep when he got there. The nurse, a tall woman with reading glasses on a beaded chain, said she'd had a good morning. Ate half a muffin. Asked twice what day it was, which was better than yesterday.

He sat in the plastic chair by the window and opened the paper he'd bought at the corner store. He didn't read it. He just held it, because holding something made him feel less like a person waiting for bad news.

Around two she woke up and looked at him for a while.

"You need a haircut," she said.

"I know."

"Your father never needed haircuts. He just had that same head of hair his whole life, like a hat."

Dev laughed, and she smiled like she'd meant to make him laugh, which maybe she had. Then she asked what day it was. He told her Thursday. She nodded and closed her eyes again, and he went back to holding the paper.

On the walk home the bus passed him at the third block. He didn't wave it down.
