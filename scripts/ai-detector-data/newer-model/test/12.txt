Grandpa kept bees for thirty years and got stung maybe once a week the whole time. He said you stop noticing after a while. I didn't believe him until the summer I turned twelve and he let me help with the hives, and by August I'd been stung so many times my arms looked like I had chicken pox, and I honestly couldn't tell you which ones hurt.

What I remember more is the smell. Smoke from the little tin smoker, and under that the honey, warm and sort of sharp, like flowers that had gone a bit too far. He'd pull a frame out and hold it up to the light and the comb would glow orange, and the bees would just keep walking around on it like nothing had happened.

He didn't talk to them, which surprised me. I'd read somewhere that beekeepers talk to their bees. When I asked, he said the bees had enough going on without listening to him.

He sold the hives when his hands got too shaky. A young couple from two towns over bought them. They sent him a jar of honey every Christmas until he died, with a note that said the bees were doing fine.

I still have the last jar. It's crystallized now, gone pale and solid. I keep meaning to warm it up and use it, and I keep not doing it.
