Every writer I know has a drawer. Sometimes it's a real drawer and sometimes it's a folder on a laptop called OLD or MISC or, in one friend's case, NO. It's where the stories go when they stop working.

Mine has about forty things in it. A novel I quit at chapter nine. A bunch of poems from college that rhyme way too hard. A short story about a man who sells umbrellas in a town where it never rains, which I wrote in one night and was sure was brilliant, and which is actually just one joke stretched over twelve pages.

I used to think the drawer was a graveyard. Now I think it's more like a parts bin. Last year I pulled a character out of that dead novel, a grumpy bus driver named Lou, and dropped him into something new. He fit. He'd been waiting.

The point, if there is one, is that nothing you write is wasted, even the bad stuff. Especially the bad stuff, because that's where you figured out what doesn't work. You can't get to the good drafts without writing the ones that go in the drawer.

So I don't throw anything out anymore. I just open the drawer, take a look around, and close it again. Lou's still in there, technically. I gave him a copy.
